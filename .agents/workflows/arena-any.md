# /arena-any

1. **Read** the challenge twice. Name the format, length limits, audience and how it will be judged if stated.
2. **Human first.** Ask the human, with defaults: their own three ideas (rough is fine), one true detail or memory, three things it must not be, the one feeling or action the audience should leave with.
3. **Inventory** 8 or more specifics from the challenge and the human: names, places, numbers, objects, sensory details, local words. Mark assumptions.
4. **Diverge.** Think step by step, then list 10 one-line angles with a rough probability each. Mark the two most probable as the rut. Run `node .agents/skills/art-direction/scripts/deal.mjs --mode any` (or roll the dice table in START-HERE.md) and fuse the dealt forms with the inventory.
5. **Three takes** that differ in form, angle and voice: one anchored in the specifics, one in a dealt form, one deliberately plain. For each: a title, the idea in one sentence, the first two lines or a thumbnail description, what it gives up. Recommend one. Stop and wait.
6. **Produce** the chosen take. Use only real facts. For image prompts: subject, setting, composition, lens or medium, light, palette from the inventory, and what to exclude (stock gradients, glossy plastic look, generic smiling people, centred symmetry unless chosen).
7. **Critique** with this rubric, 1 to 10 each: originality (could a competitor's AI have written it?), specificity (concrete details only this brief has), craft (rhythm, structure, economy), fit (format, audience, constraints), human presence (an opinion, a memory, a risk). Check copy against the copy-voice tells. Fix the top three problems; show before and after.
8. **Human signature.** The human rewrites the title, the opening line and the closing line in their own words. Apply exactly.
9. **Process note**: what the human contributed, what the AI generated, what was rejected, the prompts used.
