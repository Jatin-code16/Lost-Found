# Anti-Slop Floor

A tell is a choice nobody made: a pattern that appears whatever the subject. It is evidence of a default, not proof a model made the page, and human designers ship these too. Avoiding them is a floor, not a strategy. A page can pass every item and still be generic.

## Why ban lists decay

Every published "use this instead" becomes the next default within months. Anthropic's own frontend-design skill now lists the "tasteful" escapes as tells, Taste Skill keeps its list dated, and avoid-ai-design notes a third, handmade wave forming. So this file never names a replacement font or colour. Replacements are procedures: derive from the subject, deal a form, choose.

## Wave 1 (2023 to 2024): the median

- Inter, Roboto, Arial, Open Sans, Lato or system-ui as the only face.
- Purple or indigo to blue gradients; indigo buttons.
- Gradient text. Coloured glows. Glass panels.
- Hero, then three icon cards, then stats, testimonials, pricing, CTA.
- Identical cards with an icon in a rounded square. Cards in cards.
- Emoji as icons. Bounce easing. Fade-up on every section.
- "Seamless", "supercharge", "unlock", "elevate", "revolutionize".

## Wave 2 (2025): the designed-looking chrome

- Eyebrow or kicker: a tiny tracked uppercase label above a heading.
- Pill badge above the hero headline ("New", "Introducing").
- One word of the headline in italic serif, colour, or underline.
- Bento grids of rounded tiles as the feature section.
- Near-black ground with one acid-green, lime or vermilion accent.
- Floating pill navigation with blur.
- Fake dashboards of grey bars; fake macOS window dots as the hero.
- Decorative status dots ("Available for work", pulsing "live").

## Wave 3 (2025 to 2026): the fix that became the look

- Warm cream ground (around #F4F1EA) + high-contrast serif + terracotta or clay accent (around #D97757). This is the most recognisable "tasteful AI" look; #D97757 is Claude's own interface colour.
- Broadsheet cosplay: hairlines everywhere, zero radius, newspaper columns on something that is not a publication.
- Template chrome: meta strings joined by middle dots (A · B · C); labels like "WORD — fragment"; monospace for small labels; a "→" glued to every link; 01 / 02 / 03 on things that are not a sequence; tinted near-black (#0B0B0B, #111) as "black".
- Emerald or teal as the accent when purple was ruled out.
- Second-wave "anti-default" fonts as the only gesture: Space Grotesk, Geist, Syne, Sora, Instrument Serif, Fraunces, Playfair, Cormorant, DM Sans, Outfit, Plus Jakarta Sans.
- Performative labels: "Field notes", "Quietly trusted by", "On our desks".

## Emerging: the anti-AI costume

Grain, hand-drawn type, scrapbook collage and "imperfect" textures used in order to look human. Legitimate when the subject earns them; the next reflex when reached for as a disguise.

## Copy tells

- The correction: "not just X, it's Y", "more than a …".
- Tricolon headlines: "Fast. Simple. Secure."
- The mad-lib: "The AI-powered platform for modern teams".
- "Welcome to …", "Meet …", "X, reimagined".
- "Get started" and "Learn more" as the only labels.
- Many em dashes. Uniform sentence rhythm. Hollow confidence.
- Invented proof: "10,000+ teams", five-star rows, Jane Doe, Acme.

## Structure tells

- Stock section order instead of the order of an argument.
- Every section the same layout; everything centred.
- One medium radius (12 to 16 px) on everything.
- A page with every tell removed and nothing put in place: the "avoidance aesthetic". Taste Skill's blind tests found a richer page with tells beat a thin clean page every time.

## Tests to run on every choice

- **Substitution test.** Did I pick this for the brief, or because it is the usual alternative to the thing I was told to avoid?
- **Category test.** Could someone guess this look from the category alone, or from the category plus this list? Then rework.
- **Competitor test.** Put the nearest competitor's name on it. If it still works, it is a category idea.
- **Mirror test.** Would a neighbouring brief in the same category get this exact combination? Change one decision.

## When a tell is fine

When the brief or the human asks for it, or the content needs it: a dashboard has dense rows, a newspaper has hairlines, a code tool shows monospace code, a real sequence gets numbers. Say why in one line. The rule targets reflex, not tools.

## Replace, never just remove

Fill the space a tell leaves with substance: larger type, a whole field of colour, a real picture of this subject's world, the full content, the one live thing. Then run the scanners again.
