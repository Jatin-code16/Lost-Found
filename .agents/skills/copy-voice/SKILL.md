# Copy and Voice

Copy can make a page look templated faster than any colour. Write the words before the markup, from the subject's own vocabulary, and leave the key lines to the human.

## 1. The copy deck (before any markup)

Answer in one line each:

- Who is reading and what do they already know?
- What is this, said the way its maker would say it to a friend?
- What one thing should the reader believe after the first screen?
- What proof would make them believe it (the thing itself, a real number, a name, a demonstration)?
- What do they do next?

Then write every section's text as plain text, in the argument order from `art-direction`.

## 2. Headlines

- A headline is a title held in one glance. Two lines or fewer at every width. Gallery heroes have a median of 5 words and 32 characters (Taste Skill study of 247 sites).
- Arithmetic: characters per line is roughly line width divided by (font size times 0.5). A phone at 390 px with a 44 px headline holds about 16 characters a line, so about 32 for two lines.
- Never fix a long headline only by shrinking it. Cut words.
- Shapes that work: the name; what it is ("Furniture for small rooms"); a claim a competitor could not sign; an instruction; the place or the moment.
- One supporting sentence under the headline says plainly what the thing is and who it is for. One primary action named for what it does ("Book a table", not "Get started").

## 3. Body, labels, states

- Specific nouns and real numbers. Plain verbs, active voice, sentence case.
- Name things by what the user understands, not how the system is built.
- An action keeps its name through the flow: "Publish" leads to "Published".
- Errors say what happened and how to fix it. Empty states invite the next action.
- Proof is never invented. Sample details for gaps (dish names, room names) are fine when listed for the human to confirm.

## 4. Tells of generated prose

Rewrite on sight. These are also the cues to look for in an "AI or human?" round, though none of them proves authorship alone.

**Words:** seamless, robust, elevate, empower, unlock, supercharge, revolutionize, leverage, delve, tapestry, testament, pivotal, vibrant, bustling, nestled, realm, journey (when nobody travels), harness, cutting-edge, game-changing, effortless, in today's fast-paced world, whether you're X or Y, let's dive in, moreover, furthermore, in conclusion.

**Moves:**
- The correction: "It's not just X, it's Y." "More than a …".
- The tricolon: three items, three beats, every time. "Fast. Simple. Secure."
- The colon headline: "Name: the X for Y".
- Hedged balance on everything ("While X, it's important to note Y").
- Every paragraph the same length; every sentence the same rhythm.
- Ending on a summary or a moral nobody asked for.
- Em dashes used as all-purpose glue, many per page.
- Abstract nouns where a concrete one exists ("solutions", "experiences", "excellence").
- Confident claims with nothing behind them.

**Signs of a human hand:** a specific number or name that could be checked; an opinion that could be wrong; uneven sentence lengths; a joke that depends on the subject; a detail too small to have been predicted.

## 5. The human signature pass

The theme rewards visible human authorship, so the human writes these themselves, at the end, in their own words:

1. The headline.
2. The first sentence of body copy.
3. The primary button label.
4. One line that only they could write (a memory, an opinion, a local word).

The agent's job: apply their text exactly, check it fits two lines, and suggest at most one cut. Do not polish their voice into yours.

## 6. Review checklist

- [ ] Read aloud: nothing sounds like a brochure.
- [ ] Competitor test: swap the name; if a line still works, make it specific.
- [ ] No tell words or moves from section 4 (or each one is deliberate).
- [ ] Every number and name is real or listed as sample content.
- [ ] Headlines two lines or fewer at 390 px.
- [ ] The human wrote the signature lines.
