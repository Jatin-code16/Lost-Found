# Prompts for any challenge (not only websites)

The loop is the same for every format: you first, wide options, you choose, produce, critique, your words last.

## The universal skeleton

```text
Challenge, exactly as given: [paste]
Format and limits: [length, medium, audience, deadline]

My notes, written before using AI:
- My own ideas: [1] [2] [3]
- One true detail or memory: [detail]
- Must not be: [a] [b] [c]
- The audience should leave feeling or doing: [x]

Step 1. Think step by step about the audience and my notes. List 10 one-line angles, each with a probability (0 to 1) of how often an AI would suggest it. Mark the two most likely as "the rut".
Step 2. Give me three takes that differ in form, angle and voice: one built on my specifics, one told as a [dealt form] with the twist [dealt twist], one deliberately plain. For each: a title, the idea in one sentence, the opening two lines, and what it gives up.
Stop there. I will choose.
```

After you choose:

```text
Write take [X]. Use only facts from my notes; if you need a detail I did not give, mark it [CHECK]. Avoid: "not just X, it's Y", tricolons, a closing moral, the words seamless, unlock, elevate, tapestry, delve, vibrant, testament, and em dashes. Vary sentence length.
```

Critique:

```text
Score it 1 to 10 on originality (could another contestant's AI have written it?), specificity, craft, fit to the brief, and human presence (an opinion, a memory, a risk). Name the top three problems with the exact line. Fix them. Show before and after.
```

## Short text: slogan, tagline, title, caption

```text
Give me 20 options in 5 groups of 4, each group a different mechanism: (1) a concrete noun from my notes, (2) a verb-first instruction, (3) a local word or phrase, (4) a number that is true, (5) a reversal of the category cliché [cliché]. Give each a probability. No rhyme unless I ask. No colon structures. Then stop.
```

## Story, poem, script

```text
Before writing, list the five most predictable choices an AI would make for this (setting, character, twist, ending, image). Avoid all five. Build the piece around my detail: [detail]. First give me three openings of two lines each in different voices. Stop.
```

## Image or poster prompt

```text
Write four image prompts that differ in composition and medium, not just colour:
1. Close crop on one object from my notes, [medium: lino print / gouache / risograph / documentary photo / blueprint].
2. Wide scene from an unusual height or angle.
3. Told as the dealt form [form], flat graphic.
4. Type-led poster, the title as the main image.
For each: subject, setting, composition, lens or medium, light, palette taken from [material in my notes], and an exclusion list: no glossy 3D render look, no purple-blue gradient, no generic smiling stock people, no lens flare, no centred symmetry unless chosen, no text unless I give it.
```

Refine one thing at a time ("same composition, light from the left, palette from the old bus ticket"). Keep the seeds or variation IDs in your log.

## Product, startup or solution idea

```text
Problem: [problem], for [specific person] in [specific place].
List 10 solution ideas with probabilities. Mark the rut. For the three least obvious that are still feasible, give: who exactly uses it, the first 60 seconds of using it, what it costs to build in a weekend, why it could fail, and one test I could run tomorrow. Do not invent market sizes or statistics.
```

## Pitch or presentation

```text
Build a 60-second pitch in five beats: the problem as one real scene; what I brought (my idea or detail); what the AI suggested and what I rejected and why; the idea in one sentence; a live demo moment. Plain speech, no hype words. Mark any number I must verify with [CHECK].
```

## "AI or human?" round

```text
For each passage, list the concrete signals for AI and for human (see the copy-voice tells), weigh them, give a confidence from 0 to 1, and say what single change would flip your verdict.
```

Do this reasoning yourself where the round forbids AI help.

## The plain-option rule

When you want the familiar result done very well, say so: "Give me the category standard, executed at the craft level of [two real examples]." That is a legitimate choice, as long as you made it on purpose.
